Motor controls training: read the ladder, find the open

Motor control circuits are the first thing most plant electricians learn to troubleshoot and the thing they troubleshoot most. ForgeLine's motor controls training covers reading the ladder, the components in the bucket, and a meter-based method for finding why a motor will not start, will not stop, or keeps tripping. These Electrical courses are free.

Read the control circuit first

Every starter has a power circuit (line, contactor, overload, motor) and a control circuit (control transformer, fuses, stop and start buttons, seal-in contact, interlocks, coil). Find both on the print before opening the door. Electrical Prints, Schematics & Ladder Diagrams covers wire numbering and cross-referencing so you can follow a signal from a field device to the coil.

Components you need to know cold

  • Contactors and starters: coil voltage, auxiliary contacts, pitted main contacts and how to inspect them.
  • Overload relays: bimetallic vs electronic, setting to motor FLA and service factor, trip class, and why an overload that "keeps tripping" usually means the motor is overloaded or single-phasing.
  • Control transformers and 24 V DC circuits: sizing, fusing, and grounding the control circuit. Control Transformers & 24 V Control Circuits.
  • Two-wire vs three-wire control: maintained contacts that restart after a power loss vs a seal-in circuit that does not.
  • Reversing, jogging and interlocks: mechanical and electrical interlocks that keep forward and reverse from closing together.
  • Reduced-voltage starting: soft starters and when a VFD is the better choice.

A meter-based troubleshooting sequence

  1. Confirm the complaint and the state of the starter (pulled in, not pulled in, chattering, tripped).
  2. With the control circuit energized and following site safety rules, measure control voltage at the transformer secondary and after the control fuse.
  3. Walk the rung: measure from the common side to each point in the ladder until the voltage disappears. The open device is between the last good point and the first dead one.
  4. If the coil has voltage but the contactor does not pull in, check the coil. If it pulls in but the motor does not run, move to the power circuit: line voltage, contacts, overload heaters, motor leads.
  5. Lock out and test the motor before replacing anything expensive.
