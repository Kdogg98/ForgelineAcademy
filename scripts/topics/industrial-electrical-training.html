Industrial electrical work is mostly three-phase power, motors, and the control circuits that start and stop them, done safely around energized equipment. ForgeLine's Electrical path takes a plant electrician from prints and safety through motor controls, VFDs, testing, and power quality. The Electrical path is free.

Safety first, and specific

Every electrical course assumes your site's program applies. Electrical Safety & Arc Flash Awareness and Electrical Safety Programs & NFPA 70E Application cover establishing an electrically safe work condition, reading arc-flash labels, test-before-touch with a verified meter, and why "it's only 120 V control" is not a safety plan. Training does not replace site LOTO or qualification.

A troubleshooting method you can defend

Electrical Troubleshooting Methodology teaches a repeatable sequence: confirm the complaint, check the print, measure at the half-split point, isolate the faulted section, fix the cause, and verify under load. Write each reading down so the fix can be explained later, not just believed.

Common plant calls and where they are covered

  • A motor trips its overload every afternoon: measure running current on all three phases against nameplate FLA, check voltage imbalance at the starter, and look at what the driven load is doing. Covered in 3-phase power and motor protection.
  • A breaker or fuse opens on start: separate a short in the motor or cable (insulation test, winding resistance) from a starting-current problem (locked rotor, wrong fuse class, mechanical bind).
  • Lights flicker or drives fault when a large motor starts: a voltage-sag and power-quality problem; measure at the panel during the start.
  • A control circuit works intermittently: loose terminals, a failing control transformer, or a damaged conductor in a flexing cable. Torque checks and infrared scans find most of these.
