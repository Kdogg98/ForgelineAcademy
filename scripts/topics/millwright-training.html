Millwright work is measured in thousandths and judged by how long the machine runs after you leave. ForgeLine's millwright training follows the Mechanical path: bearings, lubrication, and alignment first, then pumps, seals, gearboxes, drives, and condition monitoring. The Mechanical path is free.

Start with bearings, lubrication and alignment

Most rotating-equipment failures trace back to three things: the wrong lubricant or amount, contamination, and misalignment. The first course, Bearings, Lubrication & Alignment Fundamentals, covers bearing types and fits, mounting and removal without damaging the bearing, grease quantity and intervals, and shaft alignment with dial indicators and laser systems, including soft foot and thermal growth.

What the Mechanical path covers

  • Pumps and seals: pump curves and the operating point, cavitation diagnosis, mechanical seal installation and failure analysis. Pump & Mechanical Seal Maintenance, Mechanical Seals Advanced Diagnostics.
  • Power transmission: gearboxes, couplings, keys and shafts, chain and belt drives. Gearboxes & Power Transmission, Industrial Couplings, Keys & Shafts, Chain & Belt Drive Systems.
  • Precision measurement: micrometers, dial indicators, feeler gauges, and how to record as-found and as-left readings. Precision Measurement & Troubleshooting, Precision Maintenance Practices.
  • Condition monitoring: vibration analysis basics, thermography, and ultrasound for lubrication. Vibration Analysis Fundamentals, Thermography, Ultrasound & Acoustic Lubrication.
  • Systems: conveyors, compressors and compressed air, fans and blowers, heat exchangers, and hydraulics and pneumatics.
  • Safety and rigging: Rigging, Lifting & Material Handling and Machine Guarding & Mechanical LOTO.

Habits the courses push

  • Take the as-found before you touch anything: temperatures, vibration, alignment readings, seal weep, belt tension.
  • Find the cause, not just the failed part. A bearing that failed from misalignment will fail again if you only swap the bearing.
  • Write it in the work order so the next crew starts from data.

A practical order for new millwrights

  1. Week 1–2: bearings, lubrication and alignment fundamentals, plus machine guarding and mechanical LOTO.
  2. Week 3–4: precision measurement, then couplings, keys and shafts, so alignment readings and fits make sense on real equipment.
  3. Month 2: pumps and mechanical seals, gearboxes, chain and belt drives. Pair each course with a work order on the same equipment type.
  4. Month 3: vibration, thermography and ultrasound basics, so you can recognize a developing failure before it becomes a breakdown.

Experienced millwrights can skip ahead: course pages list every module and lesson with estimated minutes, so you can pick the gaps.
