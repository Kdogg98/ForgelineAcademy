Loop troubleshooting fundamentals

The analog 4-20 mA loop is still the backbone of most plants. Know the loop: power supply, transmitter, wiring, the input card, and any isolators or barriers. Measure current in series or with a clamp meter rated for milliamps, compare it with the DCS reading and the expected process value, and check whether the signal is at a fail-safe level (for example below 3.8 mA or above 20.5 mA under NAMUR NE43). Calibration you can defend

A calibration record should show the as-found readings at several points across the range (commonly 0, 25, 50, 75 and 100 percent, rising and falling), the reference standard used and its own calibration date, the tolerance, the adjustment made, and the as-left readings. If the as-found was out of tolerance, someone needs to ask what the process did while the instrument was wrong. Smart transmitters need both the sensor trim and the analog output checked; changing the range in the HART configuration is not a calibration.

Final control elements

A 4-20 mA command is not proof the valve moved. Check air supply to the positioner, bench set, travel against command, and stiction. Control valve troubleshooting, Control Valve Calibration & Diagnostics, Smart Valve Positioners.
