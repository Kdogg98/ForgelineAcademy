Hydraulics training for maintenance technicians

Hydraulic systems fail in a few predictable ways: contamination, heat, leaks, and components worn past their tolerance. ForgeLine's hydraulics training teaches maintenance technicians to read a circuit, take pressure and flow readings, and find the cause before swapping expensive parts. The core course, Hydraulics & Pneumatics Basics, is free on the Mechanical path.

Stored energy comes first

A hydraulic machine with the motor locked out can still move. Accumulators hold pressure, suspended loads and cylinders under gravity hold pressure, and trapped oil between closed valves holds pressure. Bleed accumulators to zero, block or lower suspended loads, and verify zero pressure with a gauge before opening a line. Pinhole leaks can inject oil through skin; never feel for a leak with your hand.

Reading the circuit

Learn the ISO 1219 symbols for pumps, relief valves, directional control valves, flow controls, check valves, cylinders and motors. Trace the circuit from the reservoir through the pump, relief valve and directional valve to the actuator and back to tank. Knowing where pressure is set and where flow is controlled tells you where to put the gauge.

Symptom-based troubleshooting

  • Actuator moves slowly: flow problem. Check pump output (a flow meter at the pump is ideal), internal leakage past cylinder seals or a worn valve spool, a flow control set wrong, or a relief valve partly open.
  • Actuator lacks force: pressure problem. Check relief valve setting with a gauge at the pump, then at the actuator; compare against the print.
  • Oil running hot: energy is being wasted as heat, often oil passing over the relief valve continuously, internal leakage, a fouled cooler, or a low reservoir.
  • Noisy pump: cavitation (restricted suction, clogged strainer, oil too cold or too viscous) or aeration (air drawn in at a suction fitting or shaft seal, foaming from a low reservoir).
  • Erratic or drifting motion: contaminated or sticking valves, air in the system, or a failing counterbalance or pilot-operated check valve.

Contamination control

Most hydraulic component failures are linked to contamination. Keep new oil filtered before it goes in, cap open lines and ports, change filters on differential-pressure indication rather than only by calendar, and sample oil for particle count and water. Clean is cheaper than rebuilt.
